About Me

Throughout my Computer Science program, one of the biggest things I've learned is that software engineering is much more than understanding syntax or knowing how to code a feature. When I first started programming, my main focus was getting my code to work and making sure the feature did what it was supposed to do. As I continued through the program, I began to realize how much critical thinking goes into developing software. It's important to look at the bigger picture, consider different scenarios, anticipate potential issues, and understand how one change can affect other parts of an application.

I've also learned that just because a solution works doesn't necessarily mean it's the best solution. There are other factors to consider, such as performance, security, maintainability, and how easily the application can grow over time. This has changed the way I approach problems. Instead of immediately jumping into writing code, I try to understand the problem, think through possible solutions, and consider the advantages and disadvantages of each approach.

Working on my CS-499 capstone helped reinforce this mindset. By revisiting my original CS-320 Contact Management application, I was able to look at code I had previously written and identify areas that could be improved. Enhancing the application through software engineering, algorithms and data structures, and database integration helped me better understand how these different areas work together to create a more reliable and maintainable application.
